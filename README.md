# Childhood famine exposure and late-life edentulism

Reproducible analysis code for:

> **Childhood famine exposure and late-life edentulism: evidence from
> natural experiments.**
> Baumeister S-E, McGrath C, Kocher T, Nascimento GG.
> *Journal of Clinical Periodontology* (manuscript 9424511).

A single, self-contained R script reproduces every table and figure in the
manuscript and supplement from harmonised individual-level data on ten historical
famines across Asia, Europe and the Americas (analytic *N* = 165,586 from a
harmonised pool of 205,768 records).

---

## TL;DR — what the study finds

Birth-cohort famine designs mechanically confound famine exposure with **age at
survey** (the exposed cohort is older, and edentulism rises steeply with age).
The crude reduced-form risk ratio collapses from **1.69 to 1.07** after age
adjustment. Under four pre-specified quasi-experimental designs that separate
exposure from the cohort/age gradient, the effect of childhood famine exposure
on late-life edentulism is **small and statistically null**.

| Design | Estimate |
|---|---|
| **RDiT — pooled, bias-corrected (primary)** | **+0.13 pp (95% CI −1.50, +1.77; *p* = 0.87)** |
| RDHonest (bias-aware, discrete running variable) | −1.24 pp (honest 95% CI −4.76, +2.27) |
| Region-of-birth × intensity DiD — CHARLS (unadj.) | +0.15 pp (95% CI −1.45, +1.75; *p* = 0.85) |
| Region-of-birth × intensity DiD — CLHLS (unadj.) | +2.10 pp (95% CI −0.18, +4.39; *p* = 0.07) |
| Interrupted time-series (level shift) | −4.01 pp (*p* = 0.15) |
| Three negative controls | all null (*p* ≥ 0.47) |

## The designs

1. **Regression Discontinuity in Time (RDiT)** — primary analysis. Hausman &
   Rapson (2018) framing; `rdrobust` bias-corrected CIs (Calonico–Cattaneo–
   Titiunik 2014); **honest, bias-aware CIs** for the discrete integer-birth-
   year running variable (`RDHonest`; Armstrong & Kolesár 2018); Donut RDiT;
   study-specific estimates. Applied to all ten cohorts.
2. **Within-country region-of-birth × famine-intensity DiD** — secondary. The
   Chen & Zhou (2007) intensity design in the two Chinese cohorts that record
   province of birth: CHARLS (Great Famine 1959–61) and CLHLS (Henan famine
   1942–43). **Unadjusted TWFE is the primary estimator**; adjusted TWFE and
   **Borusyak–Jaravel–Spiess (2024) imputation** are sensitivity estimators.
   Single-event 2×2 design → TWFE is unbiased (Baker et al. 2026).
3. **Interrupted time-series (ITA)** — secondary. Segmented regression with a
   level shift and slope change at the famine cohort cut-off.
4. **Negative controls** — placebo cohort cut-off, placebo treatment window
   (age 11–15), and a negative-control outcome (sex).

**Sensitivity analyses** (Supplement): descriptive statistics by study and
cohort (S1); reduced-form association before and after age adjustment (S2);
leave-one-out omitting each cohort incl. Mexico (S3); famine-duration
stratification (S4).

## Reproduce

Requires **R >= 4.5**. Install dependencies:

```r
install.packages(c("haven","dplyr","tidyr","purrr","fixest","rdrobust","rddensity",
                   "RDHonest","didimputation","sandwich","lmtest","broom","ggplot2",
                   "forcats"))
# RDHonest may need:  remotes::install_github("kolesarm/RDHonest")
```

Then, from a shell:

```bash
export FAMINE_DATA_DIR=/path/to/your/data      # Windows: set FAMINE_DATA_DIR=C:\path\to\data
Rscript famine_edentulism_analysis.R
```

The script writes all tables to `tables/`, figures to `figures/`, and
`sessionInfo.txt`. On first run it builds an individual-level pooled file
(`famine_edent_pooled.rds`, cached and **git-ignored**); delete it to force a
rebuild.

## Notes on identification

- **Exposure is located by province of birth**, not province of survey — the two
  diverge for internal migrants, and survey province would misclassify childhood
  famine exposure.
- **Age is collinear with birth year** (age = survey year - birth year). The
  region-of-birth DiD is reported without covariate adjustment; the TWFE with
  region and birth-year fixed effects absorbs the age trend. Simply entering age
  as a covariate alongside a cohort-defined exposure does **not** break this
  collinearity — a key reason conventional cohort regressions overstate famine
  effects.
